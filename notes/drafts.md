# Drafts, round 2 (session s306)

## Judge's criteria (quoted)
1. "It is a poem about agency: it is written as a poem, and its subject is asking and being given, or having, handing over or taking back the power to act."
2. "It moves the reader: an image, a turn or an ending that lands with feeling and stays after the last line."
3. "Insight: it says something true about agency that a reader recognises at once and had not quite put into words, never a slogan or a lecture."
4. "Craft: sound, rhythm and line breaks that serve the meaning; concrete, exact words, none that could be cut."
5. "Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock phrases or a familiar poem reworded."

Criterion 1 in my words: it has to read plainly as a poem (lines, compression, image), and what happens in it has to be someone asking for the power to act and someone handing it over (or taking it back). No ambiguity about either.

Brief from operator: first entry (164 cp, "Send?") ranks #209. Aim for 240-275 cp, one physical scene, 2-3 senses, feeling never named, quiet turn, last line a completed physical action. No baby, hair-rinsing, bicycle, swimming, driving.

## Drafts
D1 carving knife. Son asks to carve; father passes knife blade-first toward himself; sits in a guest's chair.
D2 tiller. "Can I steer?" Father lifts his hand off the warm tiller, goes forward to sit in the bow where he used to put me.
D3 bell tower. Old ringer hands over the treble rope; the sally rises out of her hands into mine; she steps back to the wall.
D4 night-shift handover. Nurse lifts the keys on the lanyard over her head, still warm from her neck, hangs them on mine.
D5 sourdough. Grandmother asks me to feed the starter; slides the jar across; washes her hands and dries them, done.
D6 power of attorney. Mother signs, then turns the pen round and holds it out to me by the cap.
D7 agent at the door. The agent asks "May I open the door for the courier?"; I say yes and listen to my own lock turn from another room.
D8 stage cue. Old stage manager slips the headset off and onto my ears; I hear the dark theatre breathing; she says "Go."

## Scoring (1 = all pass; then 2, 3 decide)
- D1: asking AND giving plain; turn (guest's chair) lands; insight: giving power means turning the risk toward yourself. Strong.
- D2: strong image, but boat/steering sits close to the driving scaffold. Good.
- D3: fresh, but the bell-ringing vocabulary (sally, treble) needs explaining. Medium.
- D4: tender, but the asking is weak (handover is just procedure). Medium.
- D5: quiet, a bit slight. Medium-low.
- D6: power of attorney feels legal and abstract. Medium.
- D7: agent scene fits the brand but repeats our first entry's yes-click. Medium.
- D8: vivid sound, but "Go" is a stock ending. Medium.
Top two: D1, D2.

## Revisions
D2 v1: Can I steer? He didn't answer, / lifted his hand off the tiller / and the boat went on being a boat...
D2 v2: cut "didn't answer"; ends "and sat in the bow, where he used to put me, / facing the way I chose." The ending is a state, not an action, and the steering stays close to the driving cliché.

D1 v1: ends "and pulled out a guest's chair." The turn is good but the poem ends before anyone asks again.
D1 v2: added the scissors line (he taught the safe pass) plus warm handle, steam, chair scrape. Ends "and sat down to be served": too explanatory.
D1 v3 (chosen): ends "and held out his plate." He becomes the one who asks, which closes the circle of asking and giving with a completed physical action. Senses: steam/heat, warm handle (touch), scraped chair (sound).

## Pick: D1 v3
It wins on 1 (asking and giving both on the page), and on 2 and 3 (the blade turned toward himself is the true insight: handing over power means taking on the risk yourself; the held-out plate is a reversal shown, not said). The scene is fresh: none of the scaffolds listed above.

Fit: v3 was 296 cp, so I cut "I asked the steam, not him." (268 cp).
Final pass (criterion 4): "warm from his palm" sat after "scissors" and read as if the scissors were warm. I moved the warmth to "bone handle warm from his palm" and made him wipe the knife instead. 267 cp.
