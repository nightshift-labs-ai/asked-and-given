# Drafts: the poem about permission

## Judge's criteria (quoted)
1. "It is a poem about agency: it is written as a poem, and its subject is asking and being given, or having, handing over or taking back the power to act."
2. "It moves the reader: an image, a turn or an ending that lands with feeling and stays after the last line."
3. "Insight: it says something true about agency that a reader recognises at once and had not quite put into words, never a slogan or a lecture."
4. "Craft: sound, rhythm and line breaks that serve the meaning; concrete, exact words, none that could be cut."
5. "Freshness: its own voice and images, not stock phrases or a familiar poem reworded."

Criterion 1 in my words: clearly verse, and plainly about asking and being given, or handing over or taking back the power to act. Binary; meet it plainly.

## Drafts
1. Kerb
She asks to cross alone.
I say yes, then stand at the kerb
with my hand still out,
holding the shape of hers.

2. Prompt
Allow once, or always?
The agent waits on my answer
the way I waited at my father's door,
keys warm in my fist,
already knowing where I'd drive.

3. Key
I cut you a key.
For a week you knocked anyway.
The day you let yourself in
I heard it from the kitchen,
the small turn I had given away.

4. Borrowed car
You lend me the car and change nothing back.
All month your seat is set to my legs,
your mirror full of my road.

5. Pen
My mother signs the paper that lets me sign for her.
Her name, slow, one last time.
She hands me the pen
and keeps her hand curled, as if it were still there.

6. Run it
"Run it," I typed, and went to make tea.
When I came back the work was done
and I stood with the cold cup, deciding
whether to read what I had let it do.

7. Door
He holds the door I could open myself.
I go through anyway.
Some gifts are only given
if somebody takes them.

8. Ask me first
Each night I tick the box: ask me first.
Each morning it asks, and I say yes,
yes, yes, until the asking
is the only part I do.

## Scores (C1 / C2 / C3 / C4 / C5, out of 5)
1. Kerb 5/4/4/4/4: one scene, ending image; human only.
2. Prompt 5/3/3/3/3: two scenes, the ending is soft.
3. Key 5/4/3/4/4: good sound, the insight is slight.
4. Car 4/3/3/4/4: agency is implied, weaker on C1.
5. Pen 5/4/4/3/4: strong; first line is wordy.
6. Run it 5/3/4/3/3: ends on an idea, not an image.
7. Door 5/2/3/3/2: ends on a moral, so it fails the brief.
8. Ask me first 5/3/4/4/3: clever, ends abstract.

Best two: 1 (Kerb) and 5 (Pen).

## Revisions
Kerb r1 (joins the agent prompt to the kerb, for readers who work beside agents):
The agent asks: Send it?
I click yes,
and my hand stays on the mouse
the way it stayed out at the kerb
the first day my daughter crossed alone,
holding the shape of hers.

Kerb r2 (cut "it", "first", tighten):
The agent asks: Send?
I click yes
and leave my hand on the mouse,
the way it stayed out at the kerb
the morning my daughter crossed alone,
holding the shape of hers.

Pen r1:
My mother signs the form that lets me sign for her.
She hands me the pen
and her fingers stay curled
around where it was.

Pen r2:
Mother signs the form that lets me sign for her,
hands me the pen,
and her fingers stay curled
around where it was.

## Pick
Kerb r2. Both meet C1. On C2 Kerb r2 wins: the late turn from the mouse to the kerb lands on a physical image ("holding the shape of hers"). On C3 it says something true: saying yes doesn't make the hand let go, because granting is a kind of holding. It also covers both a person and an AI agent, which is the brief's subject. Pen r2 is close but darker and narrower.
